01

Beauty Advisor & Assurance

Zalando · 0→1 · live iOS DE

What I built

A skincare advisor working in two modes. Assurance (lives on PDP) gives a match or no-match verdict on the product page, with the reason. In case of no-match, Assurance also provides recommendations with the current PDP as anchor product.

Advisor (lives on profile) runs a guided diagnostic and provides personalized recommendations listing the top 5 products for the user from the whole skincare assortment, across 7 categories.

Next step is to build Anticipator, to anticipate customer needs ahead of time with recommendations.

Why it was hard

Skincare is a hard and extremely risky category to solve the recommendation problem for. The problem is genuine and deep, but the risks of recommending products are equally high.

Suitability is not one score. It combines skin-type fit, concern efficacy and product specification fit across several dimensions, including customer review signals, based on imperfect data. The logic for generating ranks, correcting imperfect data by creating a parallel database, and the challenge of ranking good products against each other, is a tough one.

The system has to generate trust — we need explainability for each recommendation. Every rationale we show is provable through ingredient analysis and is not a marketing message. In a skin-health category, a plausible-but-wrong answer costs more trust than silence.

What it moved

~15% of skincare customers expected to adopt the feature
€xx mn (9%) GMV modelled for 2027
1.8→2.5x order frequency, the strategy this serves

03

ML-driven flash sales

Blibli · shipped · machine learning

My first shipped ML product, and the start of a personalization thread that runs through everything since.

What I built

A machine-learning rebuild of the flash sale engine: which products go on sale, how deep the discount runs, and when each slot schedules — across a catalogue of more than a million SKUs.

It replaced a manual, category-manager-driven process for 300M+ customers.

Why it was hard

A flash sale is a multi-objective optimisation — margin against sell-through against traffic — run inside tight time windows across a huge catalogue. Manual curation could not hold all three at once, and the merchandising team had to trust a model with the calls they used to make themselves.

The redesign, in shape

Five structural changes turned a manual, calendar-blind process into one a machine could run — and a human could still steer.

REDESIGN PILLARS5 structural changes
Surface One unified product feed, not a rigid category grid — with an optional filter, not a fixed structure
Ranking Top placement earned by discount depth, not reserved by category — merit over rotation
Ownership One accountable coordinator role with explicit CTR, conversion and GMV targets — not a shared, untracked responsibility
Budgeting A monthly calendar planned and shared ahead of time, tracked digitally — not discovered after the spend
Allocation Budget weighted toward what customers actually want on sale, not defaulted to the highest-ticket items

What it moved

+5% overall company revenue
300M+ customers reached
1M+ SKUs priced and scheduled automatically

A

The Second Brain

Personal build · LLM-based second brain · daily use

What it is

I run most of my day through Claude Code, Gmail, Google Chat, meeting notes, Jira, and monthly business reviews. The problem was never capturing any single one of those. It was that they lived in six different places and forgot each other by the next morning.

So I built a second brain: every signal gets pulled into one Daily Note, cross-linked into a permanent Wiki, and made fully LLM-readable. Claude reads the whole graph — not a folder, not a search index — before it drafts a reply, writes a document, or opens a ticket. Nothing gets re-explained twice.

Why it earns its place

The diagram is a hub-and-spoke network, not a pipeline, on purpose. A pipeline implies information moves once, left to right, and is done. This system loops — outputs feed back into the next day's capture — which is the actual point of a second brain: it compounds.

Where it is going

Built with a small set of Claude Code skills chained together — capture, daily note, wiki, LLM context, drafts, decisions, tickets — running daily. It now answers my chat messages, drafts my emails, and writes management updates tailored to who's asking, among other things.
